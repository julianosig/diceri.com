# Move cloned voices to Inworld, test first

Diceri should replace ElevenLabs with **Inworld Realtime TTS-2 (`inworld-tts-2`)** as the default voice-cloning TTS. Keep **Mistral Voxtral Mini TTS 3** ready as the fallback. Neither switch should happen before a two-week blind evaluation on real pre-join recordings. Inworld is the only hosted provider that does all of the following at once. It sits inside ElevenLabs' confidence interval on the one independent cloned-voice arena (**Elo 1150 against 1158** for Eleven v4 Turbo). It starts speaking faster in both independent latency benchmarks (**186 ms on Artificial Analysis, 170 ms P50 on Coval**). It costs **$15–25 per million characters** against ElevenLabs' $40–50. Its pricing page lists **100 to 30,000 stored voices with no monthly creation cap and no per-clone fee**, which removes the 95-operation limit that blocked every call. Voxtral 3 is the cleaner structural fit: it accepts the reference recording inline on every request and stores no voice, so there is no quota to hit. It is also the cheapest at **$13/M**. But it ranks **89 Elo below ElevenLabs** on cloned voices, which predicts that listeners would prefer ElevenLabs about 63% of the time, and it starts audio in 455–576 ms. That makes it the fallback, not the default. The evidence has one structural weakness. Every independent measurement (arena Elo, time to first audio) is **English-only**. Every per-language number for pronunciation and speaker similarity is **vendor-run**. One such run, by Fish Audio, finds Inworld's error rate higher than ElevenLabs v4's in 19 of 24 languages. So the evaluation is not a formality: it decides whether Inworld serves every language or ElevenLabs-class quality has to be bought back for some of them. At an assumed 850 translated characters per two-person call-minute, the TTS cost falls from **$0.034–0.043 to about $0.017** with Inworld on its $25/month Creator plan, or **$0.011** with Voxtral.

## The outage came from a per-call clone quota, not from price

ElevenLabs meters voice creation separately from synthesis. The Creator plan allows **95 voice add/edit operations a month and 30 voice slots**. Pro allows 290 operations, Scale and Business 1,040, and only Enterprise is unlimited ([ElevenLabs help: add/edit limit](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/help-center/account/general/is-there-a-limit-on-how-many-times-i-can-edit-add-voices), [ElevenLabs help: voice slots](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/help-center/account/general/how-many-voice-slots-do-i-get-per-tier-and-how-can-i-increase-it)). Diceri spends one operation per participant per call. It clones at pre-join and deletes at call end, as ADR 0006 and ADR 0015 require. ADR 0014 made a recorded voice mandatory to join. Under those rules **95 operations is about 47 two-person calls a month**, and even the $990 Business plan tops out near 520. ElevenLabs' models documentation describes no way to synthesize from reference audio without creating a voice first ([ElevenLabs models](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/models)). Upgrading the plan therefore buys time, not a fix. Moving from Creator to Pro ($99) is a reasonable stopgap that roughly triples call capacity while the evaluation runs.

That reframes the selection criterion. Stored-voice slots barely matter, because Diceri deletes each clone when the call ends. What matters is **whether creating a voice is metered**, and whether a provider can skip creation entirely. Three hosted providers document true per-request cloning. **Mistral Voxtral** accepts a base64 `ref_audio` and states "the voice isn't saved" ([Mistral voice cloning](https://docs.mistral.ai/studio/audio/text-to-speech/voice-cloning)). **Fish Audio** accepts inline `references` (audio plus transcript, sent as MessagePack), though Fish itself says a pre-created model gives better quality and latency ([Fish TTS API](https://docs.fish.audio/api-reference/endpoint/openapi-v1/text-to-speech), [Fish best practices](https://docs.fish.audio/resources/best-practices/text-to-speech)). **Google Chirp 3 Instant Custom Voice** returns a client-held key with no limit on key count, but it is allow-listed through sales ([Google ICV](https://docs.cloud.google.com/text-to-speech/docs/chirp3-instant-custom-voice)). Every other credible provider creates a stored voice. The difference is in the limits. Inworld publishes slot counts per plan and no creation cap ([Inworld pricing](https://inworld.ai/pricing)). Gradium documents that deleting a voice frees its slot immediately ([Gradium limits](https://docs.gradium.ai/guides/limits.md)). MiniMax charges **$1.50 per clone** at first use ([MiniMax pricing](https://platform.minimax.io/docs/guides/pricing)). For a product that clones per call, that fee alone exceeds a ten-minute call's synthesis cost tenfold.

ADR 0015 is what makes the switch cheap. Each account's ~10 s recording is kept, encrypted, precisely so voices can be recreated at another provider. Nobody has to record again. The outage also exposes a coupling worth fixing whatever provider is chosen. ADR 0014 already says that when a provider refuses a clone, that speaker's translations fall back to captions only. A **quota error at clone time should take the same path** instead of blocking the join, so the next vendor limit degrades a call rather than stopping it.

## Three providers sit within ElevenLabs' error bars on cloned voices

The most relevant independent evidence is Artificial Analysis' **Controlled Voice arena**. Every model there clones the same eight English voices, and listeners vote blind between pairs. **Eleven v4 Turbo scores 1158 [1147–1169], Cartesia Sonic 3.6 1154, and Inworld Realtime TTS-2 1150 [1139–1161]**, so the three overlap. Alibaba's Qwen-Audio-3.1-TTS-Plus leads at 1208. Voxtral 3 (1069), Inworld TTS-2 Flash (1067), MiniMax 2.8 (1029–1031) and Fish S2 Pro (1012) trail. ElevenLabs' older low-latency models sit near the anchor: Flash v2.5 at 986 and Turbo v2.5 at 982 ([AA Controlled Voice](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-speech/leaderboard/controlled-voice)). The native-voice arena ranks Eleven v4 Turbo first at 1343, but it rewards each vendor's choice of stock voices, which Diceri never uses ([AA leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-speech/leaderboard)). A useful sanity check: an 89-Elo gap predicts that ElevenLabs v4 Turbo beats Voxtral 3 in about 63% of pairings. Voxtral beats Flash v2.5 by a similar margin. That matches Mistral's own claim of a **68.4% human-preference win over Flash v2.5** ([Voxtral paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.25551v2)), so the vendor result and the independent arena agree once you account for which ElevenLabs model each one used.

Two independent operators measure time to first audio (TTFA), including leading silence. Both use English text and stock voices. Artificial Analysis measures **Inworld TTS-2 at 186 ms, Eleven v4 Turbo 258 ms, Fish Drama 3 258 ms, Cartesia Sonic 3.6 287 ms, Voxtral 3 455–576 ms and flagship Eleven v4 653 ms** ([AlphaSignal summary of AA, 2026-11-16](https://alphasignal.ai/news/artificial-analysis-tracks-how-fast-voice-ai-actually-starts-speaking)). Coval's P50 figures, as republished by Gradium (a competitor), are Inworld Flash 75 ms, Inworld TTS-2 170 ms, ElevenLabs Flash v2.5 185 ms, Gradium 214 ms, Fish S2.1 Pro 293 ms and Cartesia Sonic 3.6 440 ms ([Gradium latency benchmark](https://gradium.ai/content/tts-latency-benchmark-2026)). The Cartesia figures differ by 150 ms between the two operators, which is a warning that region and network matter as much as the model. Neither operator tested a cloned voice or a non-English language.

Pronunciation evidence per language is entirely vendor-run. The only recent run that measured ElevenLabs v4 and Inworld TTS-2 side by side is Fish Audio's, on the MiniMax 24-language test set. It reports **Inworld's error rate higher than v4's in 19 of 24 languages**. Most gaps are modest (Portuguese 1.79 vs 1.11 WER, Japanese CER 4.23 vs 3.66, Hindi CER 7.16 vs 5.78), but Arabic doubles (CER 1.39 vs 0.71). Inworld wins Spanish and German ([Fish Drama 3 report, Table 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.18010v2)). On speaker similarity the vendor tables point the other way: every competitor that measured it, including MiniMax, Mistral and Alibaba, beats older ElevenLabs models by 0.07–0.3 cosine ([MiniMax-Speech](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07916), [Voxtral paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.25551v2), [Qwen3-TTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.15621)). One independent listener study also rated ElevenLabs clones **significantly less similar** to the original speakers than MiniMax or AnyVoice clones, while finding them more intelligible ([arXiv 2604.01562](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01562v1)). Read together, this suggests ElevenLabs trades identity for clean, standard-sounding output. For a product whose promise is "your voice", that trade-off is not obviously the right one. No one has published speaker similarity for v4 itself.

## Inworld wins on the combination; Voxtral wins on structure

**Inworld TTS-2 is the primary recommendation** because it is the only option that is close to ElevenLabs on cloned-voice quality, faster, at least 2x cheaper, and broad enough in languages at the same time. Its 94 languages in Tiers 1–2 cover all twelve core languages and most of the ~57 Diceri supports. It is also the only provider that publishes honest quality tiers, which tells us exactly where to listen hardest ([Inworld multilingual](https://docs.inworld.ai/tts/capabilities/multilingual)). It lists zero data retention for both TTS-2 and Flash ([Inworld models](https://docs.inworld.ai/tts/tts-models)). It also fits the existing code. Inworld's clone-then-synthesize flow (`POST /voices/v1/voices:clone` returns a `voiceId`) maps one-to-one onto the current `VoiceCloner` create-on-join, delete-on-leave port, so the swap is two new adapters in `crates/infra/src/synthesis/` and a composition-root change. Flash is a second lever inside the same account: about half the price and much faster, at a clear quality cost (Elo 1067). Consider it only for languages where the evaluation shows it holds up.

Inworld has two documented weaknesses, and the evaluation must quantify both. First, pronunciation: the vendor-run Fish table puts it behind v4 in most languages, with Arabic the clearest gap. Second, accent policy. Inworld clones give "their best speaker similarity in the language [they were] cloned in". In other languages the model deliberately aims for a native accent, and accent "localization" currently works only for English-source voices ([Inworld multilingual](https://docs.inworld.ai/tts/capabilities/multilingual)). For a pt-BR speaker heard in Japanese, that means native Japanese pronunciation at some cost to how much it sounds like them. That is arguably the right product trade, but it has to be heard, not assumed. One practical risk is that Inworld's cloning documentation is behind a login, so nobody has yet confirmed in writing that there is no clone-creation rate limit. Day one of the evaluation must check that, because a hidden cap would repeat the ElevenLabs failure.

**Voxtral Mini TTS 3 is the fallback** because it eliminates the failure class entirely. There is no voice object, so there is nothing to meter, nothing to delete at call end, and no retention question for ADR 0006. Mistral offers EU endpoints and customer-controlled retention ([Mistral Voxtral 3](https://mistral.ai/news/voxtral-3/)), and at $13/M it is the cheapest hosted option. It falls short of primary on two independent measures: 89 Elo behind ElevenLabs on cloned voices, and 2–3x Inworld's TTFA. There are also three unknowns. Its full 35-language list was not retrieved, so ja, ko, zh and ru coverage is unconfirmed. Its TTS rate limits are unpublished. And it was released on 2026-10-20, with some sources calling it a public preview. Using it needs one port change. `SpeechRequest` currently carries only a `VoiceId`, so the reference sample (or a handle the agent can resolve to it) must reach the synthesizer. An alternative is to use Mistral's saved voices with a one-day `retention_notice` and keep the current port.

The other options lose on specific grounds. **Cartesia** ties on quality but is barely cheaper than ElevenLabs, and it builds each clone from one language, so cross-lingual output needs an extra "Add Voice Accents" step per voice ([Cartesia cloning](https://docs.cartesia.ai/build-with-cartesia/capability-guides/clone-voices)). **Fish** has the right API shape. But its strongest model in the arena (Drama 3, 1072) costs $50 per million *bytes*, the $15 S2.1 Pro scores lower, inline references are explicitly its lower-quality mode, and its consent, retention and residency terms were not verified. **Qwen-Audio-3.1** has the best cloned-voice Elo and the strongest published CJK cross-lingual results. It lacks Hindi, runs from Asian regions, and would mean a second clone per speaker. It stays what the earlier multilingual research made it: a conditional route for zh/ja/ko targets, switched on only if the evaluation shows a clear gain. **MiniMax's** per-clone fee, **Gradium's** five languages and **Google's** sales gate rule them out for now.

### Self-hosting does not pay yet

Commercially licensed open models now report speaker similarity above older ElevenLabs models. Qwen3-TTS (Apache-2.0) beats Multilingual v2 on SIM in all ten of its languages ([Qwen3-TTS report](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.15621)). VoxCPM2 (Apache-2.0, 30 languages) took the best overall rank in a human cross-lingual study ([Confucius4-TTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.11650v1)). Both figures come from vendor papers. The blockers are operational. Qwen3-TTS lacks Hindi and Arabic. Its vLLM-Omni integration is offline-only, with streaming listed as future work ([Qwen3-TTS GitHub](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS)). The open Qwen3 TTS ranks 96th (Elo 932) on the native-voice arena. A warm GPU costs **$250–650 a month** at RunPod's $0.34–0.89/h for an L4 or RTX 4090 ([RunPod pricing](https://www.runpod.io/pricing)), and by our estimate serves only 3–5 simultaneous speakers per replica. At Inworld's $20/M, that floor equals 12.5–32.5M characters a month, roughly **15,000–38,000 two-person call-minutes**, before anyone is paid to run the GPUs. The strongest multilingual open models (Fish S2, OmniVoice, Voxtral open weights, Higgs v3, F5-TTS) are non-commercial, and Chatterbox has been unmaintained since August 2026 ([Chatterbox](https://github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox)). Self-hosting becomes worth revisiting when monthly volume passes that break-even, or when a provider's data terms become unacceptable.

At roughly 14 characters per second, that is **about 850 characters**. One participant's speech is always being re-spoken for the other. The figures are our arithmetic on list prices. The clone-fee row is the real lesson: a per-clone charge, which looks negligible on a pricing page, can cost more than all synthesis combined when clones last one call.

## Risks the public evidence cannot rule out

The largest risk is **language-specific regression**. All independent quality and latency data is English. Diceri's users hear pt-BR, Japanese, Hindi and Arabic, and the only multilingual comparison that includes Inworld is run by a competitor (Fish Audio). Second is **cross-lingual identity**. No provider publishes speaker similarity for a pt-BR voice speaking Japanese, and research on accent leakage shows that suppressing a source accent can cost timbre ([arXiv 2610.07524](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.07524)). Third is **reference quality**. Public tests use clean Common Voice or studio clips, while Diceri's references are 10 s browser recordings. Fourth is **hidden quotas**. Inworld's cloning docs are login-gated, and Mistral publishes no TTS rate limits. Inworld's concurrency (10 requests on Creator, 50 on Builder) is also tighter than ElevenLabs' at similar spend. Fifth is **data handling**. Inworld's hosting region was not found, so `docs/data-inventory.md` needs a transfer basis under LGPD and GDPR before production audio flows. Sixth is **consent scope**. ADR 0015 says the kept recording is used only to recreate the person's voice for their own translations. Sending real users' recordings to five vendors for a bake-off is a different purpose. The evaluation should use team members and volunteers who sign a specific evaluation consent, not the production `voice_samples` table. Finally, **vendor churn is real**. Hume shut its TTS API in November 2026, and Mistral deprecated the first Voxtral TTS within seven months. The kept-recording design (ADR 0015) and a tested fallback adapter are the mitigation, so maintain both.

## A two-week blind evaluation decides the switch

**Days 1–2: contract checks before any listening.** Open paid accounts with Inworld (Creator) and Mistral. Script 300 create-then-delete clone cycles in one day against Inworld and confirm that no quota or rate error appears and that deletion frees the slot. Confirm in writing: no monthly creation cap, the delete endpoint, consent and training-use terms, reference-audio retention, hosting region, and that output can be 24 kHz s16 mono PCM, which the `TextToSpeech` port requires (Mistral's `pcm` is reportedly float32). Any failed check removes that provider from the evaluation.

**Days 2–4: corpus.** Recruit **16 consenting speakers**, gender-balanced, with two native speakers each of pt-BR, en, es, fr, de, ja, hi and ar. Record their references **through the production pre-join flow** (same browser capture, same 10 s sentence, same speech checks), so the test sees the audio the product actually sends. Build **40 target sentences per language** by running real call-style source utterances through the production translator. Include numbers, dates, names, brands, short backchannels ("Sim.", "OK") and one-word turns, because those break TTS more often than clean prose. Each speaker is synthesized into their own language (same-language) and into three targets (cross-lingual): en, es, and one distant language (ja, zh, hi or ar, rotated), which covers all twelve core languages.

**Days 4–6: systems and automatic metrics.** Generate every item with **ElevenLabs v4 Turbo as the control**, Inworld TTS-2, Inworld TTS-2 Flash and Voxtral 3 (inline `ref_audio`). Add Qwen-Audio-3.1-Plus for zh/ja/ko targets only. Score four things. Intelligibility: CER/WER by back-transcription with two ASR systems (Soniox `stt-rt-v6` and Whisper large-v3), reported separately because the Voxtral paper used its own vendor's ASR. Speaker similarity: cosine against the reference with one fixed speaker-verification model (WavLM-large-SV), reported only as a difference from ElevenLabs on the same item, because absolute SIM scales differ between models. Failure rate: an utterance counts as a failure when back-transcription differs by more than 20%, or when it is truncated, babbles or repeats. TTFA: from the production host, measure from request send to the first PCM sample above −40 dBFS, at P50 and P95, over 200 requests per system per language. For Voxtral the reference upload is included. A 30-minute soak at 20 concurrent streams measures error rate.

**Days 6–12: blind listening.** Use native raters for each target language: the team for pt-BR, en and es, and a paid panel (for example Prolific) for the others. Rate in two tasks. In the **similarity task**, raters hear the speaker's real reference, then two anonymized, loudness-normalized clips (ElevenLabs vs. a candidate, in random order). They pick which sounds more like the person, or "no difference", and rate each clip 1–5 on "same person?". In the **naturalness task**, raters hear the same pair without the reference and judge native pronunciation and naturalness. Collect at least **100 paired judgments per candidate per core language and 400 per candidate overall**. At that size the 95% confidence interval on a win rate is about ±5 points overall.

**Pass/fail thresholds.** These are our proposals, not values from the sources. A candidate passes as the default only if every row holds:

| Metric (candidate vs. ElevenLabs v4 Turbo control) | Pass |
|---|---|
| Blind similarity preference, all languages (win + half of ties) | Lower 95% bound ≥ 40%, i.e. not clearly worse |
| Blind similarity preference, any single core language | ≥ 35% |
| Blind naturalness/pronunciation preference, each core language | ≥ 35%; overall lower bound ≥ 40% |
| "Same person?" mean score | Within 0.3 points of ElevenLabs (1–5 scale), cross-lingual included |
| Automatic SIM difference, median per language | ≥ −0.03 |
| CER/WER per core language (both ASRs agree) | ≤ ElevenLabs + 1.0 point absolute and ≤ 1.5× ElevenLabs |
| Numbers/names/dates subset error | ≤ ElevenLabs + 5 points |
| Failure rate (truncation, babble, repetition) | ≤ 0.5% of utterances |
| TTFA from production host | P50 ≤ 300 ms, P95 ≤ 600 ms |
| Soak at 20 concurrent streams | ≥ 99.5% success; zero quota errors |
| Effective cost from invoices | ≤ $25 per 1M characters |

The outcomes map directly to decisions. If Inworld TTS-2 passes everywhere, it becomes the sole default. If it fails only in specific languages, the decision depends on where. A failure in zh/ja/ko, with Qwen-Audio passing there, is handled through the existing per-language routing table. A failure elsewhere means those targets either fall back to Voxtral if it passes, or keep ElevenLabs on a small paid plan. ElevenLabs' operation cap then binds only for that slice of traffic. If Inworld fails overall and Voxtral passes, Voxtral becomes the default. If both fail, the fallback is ElevenLabs Scale or Enterprise, and quota-to-captions degradation should ship anyway.

**Days 12–14 and beyond: canary.** Ship the winning adapter behind a provider setting (for example `DICERI_TTS_PROVIDER`). Add contract tests that run the existing `VoiceCloner` and `TextToSpeech` suites against it, plus a live-provider test in `crates/infra/tests/live_providers.rs`. Route 10% of new calls to it for a week and compare clone failures, TTFA, in-call drop-off and any user feedback against ElevenLabs. Only then move all traffic, and keep the ElevenLabs adapter and key for two weeks as a rollback. An ADR should record the provider change, which superseding the ElevenLabs choice in [ADR 0005](../../decisions/0005-speech-translation-provider-stack.md) requires.

## Conclusion

The most useful lesson is about the shape of the pricing, not the quality ranking. A voice-translation product that clones per call needs either per-request cloning or unmetered creation. ElevenLabs offers neither below Enterprise, and MiniMax's per-clone fee fails the same way through cost. Seen that way, the field narrows quickly to Inworld and Mistral. The quality question then turns out to be smaller than expected: once the comparison uses cloned voices instead of each vendor's stock voices, ElevenLabs' lead shrinks to within noise of Inworld and Cartesia. The independent evidence also suggests ElevenLabs gives up resemblance to the speaker for clean, standard-sounding output.

What the public record cannot say is whether that parity survives outside English, with laptop-microphone references, across distant language pairs. That is precisely Diceri's operating condition, so the in-house test is the real decision instrument, and the published benchmarks are only a shortlist. Because ADR 0015 kept the recordings, the test costs a few hundred dollars and two weeks. It does not require asking a single user to record again.
