# Diceri technical review · 25 November 2026

**Keep LiveKit and the three proposed providers for the pilot. Change the architecture to support both a cascade and a managed translation path, and validate the worker in Python before committing to Rust.** Palabra and DeepL Aure are the first managed challengers I would test. Launch paid voice translation on a small set of verified language pairs. Diceri’s actual browser latency, cross-language speaker likeness, and cost under load remain unmeasured. [LiveKit translator recipe](https://docs.livekit.io/reference/recipes/pipeline_translator/) · [Palabra API](https://www.palabra.ai/voice-translation-api) · [DeepL Aure release](https://www.deepl.com/en/press-release/aure-voice-ai-model)

## 1. Architecture and latency

**Keep the SFU and change the worker design.** Give each human microphone track one ingest and recognition stream. Share recognition across targets as the product grows, and generate audio once per speaker, target locale, and voice mode. Keep translation workers out of the human roster in the UI. A LiveKit `hidden` publisher suppresses participant updates that clients need for normal track discovery. Use a visible service participant, enforce the two-human limit separately, and grant subscriptions server-side where access needs to be restricted. Prototype with LiveKit’s Python translation recipe. Keep axum as the control plane and choose Rust for the worker if profiling or operational requirements justify the additional implementation. [LiveKit server source](https://github.com/livekit/livekit/blob/master/pkg/rtc/room.go#L1452) · [Track subscriptions and permissions](https://docs.livekit.io/transport/media/subscribe/) · [Python translator recipe](https://docs.livekit.io/reference/recipes/pipeline_translator/) · [Rust SDK](https://github.com/livekit/rust-sdks)

**Budget for conversation, including the time spent waiting for a clause.** The listed components add up to **0.85–2.0 seconds from phrase end to first audio**. A long source clause, accumulated playback, or a reconnected provider session can make the listener wait considerably longer. The cited LiveLingo comparison uses different endpoints across its reported timings, including first audio, finished audio, and final text. It provides no head-to-head result for Diceri’s proposed stack. Measure source word or clause to corresponding *audible* translation at p50 and p95, plus first audio, completion lag, queue age, and skipped content. Use two real browsers on speakers, headsets, mobile networks, and TURN. [Diceri latency budget](docs/architecture.md:58) · [LiveLingo benchmark materials](https://github.com/livelingo/voice-translation-benchmark) · [Long-form speech-to-speech evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15059)

Implement stable clause commits with bounded lookahead, independent full-duplex directions, and a maximum playout age. A brief “yeah” from the listener should leave the other direction intact. A language change or reconnect should invalidate old audio and prevent replay. Keep original audio as a separate track, duck it during actual translated playout, and expose an original-audio control. Test acoustic echo on speakerphones and keep translated tracks out of the recognition input. [Soniox endpointing](https://docs.soniox.com/stt/rt/endpoint-detection) · [Soniox v6 echo filtering](https://soniox.com/blog/soniox-stt-v6) · [OpenAI listening and ducking guidance](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/voice_solutions/realtime_translation_guide)

## 3. Routing and fallbacks

Keep one small capability table. Use declared speech language as an STT hint, allow code-switching, and bypass translation for speech already in the listener’s language. Key MT exceptions by **directed pair and locale**, and TTS exceptions by **reference language, output locale, and voice mode**. Remove the blanket Tier 2 Flash upgrade. AssemblyAI is a valid coverage candidate for Cantonese, Zulu and Xhosa **input**. Qualify output separately. Xhosa is absent from ElevenLabs’ published v4 list, and the Fish Audio test reports **19.74% Cantonese CER for v4**. [Soniox language ID](https://docs.soniox.com/stt/concepts/language-identification) · [AssemblyAI languages](https://www.assemblyai.com/products/streaming-speech-to-text) · [ElevenLabs language list](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/models) · [Fish Audio Table 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.18010v2)

Fail over at clause boundaries under one deadline. Keep original audio available, label a stock-voice switch, and send a reference to a backup vendor only when covered by consent. Scribe is a viable STT backup, but pairing it with ElevenLabs TTS concentrates outage exposure. Use Gemini or AssemblyAI as an independent backup for qualified pairs. Publish provisional captions if helpful and replace them with the text actually spoken once committed. Load-test warm sockets against plan limits. ElevenLabs meters open Dialogue WebSockets against a separate session pool. [ElevenLabs concurrency docs](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/models#text-to-dialogue-concurrency) · [ElevenLabs incident history](https://status.elevenlabs.io/history) · [Soniox streaming translation](https://docs.soniox.com/stt/rt/real-time-translation)

## 4. Paid-product risks and changes

- **Conversation quality and trust.** Start with EN, ES and pt-BR as a proposed cohort. Require native review in each direction on overlaps, accented and noisy speech, and corrections involving names, amounts and negation. Test speaker recognition separately from accent naturalness. Compare the *whole call* against Meet’s **63-language** translation, Teams’ real-time voice simulation, and Palabra. KUDO and Wordly are useful benchmarks for glossary and event features. Diceri’s clearest initial advantage is a guest joining by link and getting a reliable two-way conversation immediately. [Google Meet rollout](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/real-time-speech-translation-in-google-Meet-expands-to-63-languages-with-automatic-language-detection.html) · [Teams Interpreter](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/teams/copilot/interpreter-in-microsoft-teams-meetings-and-calls) · [Palabra](https://www.palabra.ai/voice-translation-api) · [KUDO](https://kudo.ai/pricing-plans-and-features/) · [Wordly](https://www.wordly.ai/wordly-platform)
- **Margin.** At two STT audio minutes, 850 synthesized characters total and $0.002 for MT, the cascade comes to **$0.040 per room-minute** in provider usage. With 425–1,275 characters under the same assumptions, it is **$0.023–$0.057**, before media, compute, retries and unused synthesis. On two continuously fed directions, Gemini’s published rates imply **about $0.042** with one total minute of translated output, and OpenAI’s rate implies **$0.068** for two billed audio minutes. Meter real usage by pair, including silence and overlap. At the base case, a 70% gross-margin target implies a price floor of about **$0.133/minute before those additional costs**. [Soniox pricing](https://soniox.com/pricing) · [ElevenLabs pricing](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing/api) · [Gemini pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing#gemini-3.5-live-translate-preview) · [OpenAI pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-realtime-translate) · [LiveKit billing](https://docs.livekit.io/deploy/admin/billing/)
- **Clone lifecycle.** Keep per-call opt-in and clear disclosure. Add immediate revocation, a durable deletion job with retries and reconciliation after worker crashes, and written verification of provider retention and clone capacity. ElevenLabs offers a delete endpoint and explicitly says voice cloning requires storing the clone even under its zero-retention program. [ElevenLabs deletion API](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/api-reference/voices/delete) · [ElevenLabs retention terms](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-api/resources/zero-retention-mode)
